x402 guards: what they charge and how fast they answer

Measured 2026-11-25 from one machine in Spain. One sample per endpoint, empty POST to the paid route. This is a snapshot, not a quality ranking.

If your agent is about to spend, every x402 "guard" answers the same first question: an unpaid request returns HTTP 402 with a price. The difference is what it costs, how fast it answers, and what it can actually decide. We probed the guard endpoints listed on x402scan with an empty body and recorded the challenge.

Latency is the round trip to the endpoint's edge, not the service's internal work. A guard on a worker edge (78 ms) and a guard behind a tunnel (80 ms) look the same from here; the second one pays two hops.

Check any x402 endpoint yourself

The challenge is the first thing any agent sees, and you can read it with one request:

curl -s -X POST https://<host>/<paid-route> \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' -d '{}' | head -c 400

A v2 answer carries accepts[0].amount (USDC units, 6 decimals), accepts[0].network, accepts[0].payTo and extra.name/version (the EIP-712 domain). If the body has no accepts, it is not an x402 v2 paywall.

What the price does not tell you

  • What it can decide. A per-call check answers one question; a batch guard answers a set of limit changes atomically (all applied or none).
  • State. A stateless check cannot enforce a daily budget across calls; it needs per-tenant state and a circuit breaker.
  • Free trial. A buyer without a wallet converts on a free call, not on a cheaper price. A2P-32 gives 10 free calls a day per IP that grow with consecutive use.
  • Failure mode. When a violation trips a lock, ask what happens next: a 402 with a reason, or a silent failure.
